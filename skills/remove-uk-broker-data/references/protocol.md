# Removal protocol

## Source controls and scope

Opting out of the open electoral register limits a source feed but does not retract old directory copies. Preserve voting registration and distinguish the full register. Confirm the user's actual current electoral address instead of reusing historical matching addresses.

TPS covers eligible live marketing calls, MPS eligible addressed marketing mail; neither erases directories or stops every unwanted contact. Residential TPS needs no routine renewal. Ex-directory requires the actual telephone provider/account and current number. Truecaller needs the actual phone number and may require account deactivation.

Keep residential directories, marketing profiles, work contacts, statutory records and technical advertising identifiers distinct. An opt-out on a cloud browser does not cover the user's phone/home browser. Do not send home addresses instead of device IDs to adtech providers.

Article 21 objections are absolute for direct marketing and associated profiling. Article 17 erasure is conditional; minimal suppression can prevent reintroduction. Do not demand deletion of necessary credit records or assume every US-only site is subject to UK GDPR.

Use a calendar-month response baseline. Clamp shorter months, move weekend/public-holiday deadlines to the next working day, and account for genuinely necessary identity information and valid notified extensions. Keep dates provisional until evidence and applicable holidays are checked. Refresh ICO guidance before escalation; it is under review following the Data (Use and Access) Act.

## Request outline

Identify the subject and specific matching listing/address or supplied identifier. State the processing purposes objected to, request marketing cessation and relevant erasure, and permit a minimal suppression record. Specify marketing-only CRA scope. Ask for confirmation, lawful retention reasons and proportionate identity checks. Describe old addresses as matching information, not current residence. Do not invent marketing received or records held.

Keep receipts, message references and evidence private. Use text receipts when screenshots could expose personal data or an approval review blocks disclosure; do not work around that rejection.
